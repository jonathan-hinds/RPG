# Icon Categories

## Active ability

Communicate an action in progress.

Use:
- Strong direction or impact.
- A hand, weapon, projectile, creature feature, or elemental force performing the action.
- Motion trails or a visible contact point.
- Exaggerated perspective.

Avoid:
- Static equipment catalog poses.
- Vague clouds of energy with no concrete action.

## Buff

Communicate empowerment, protection, restoration, readiness, or enhancement.

Useful motifs:
- Radiant shield.
- Glowing weapon.
- Reinforced armor.
- Upward motion.
- Halo, wing, hand, heart, leaf, or stable aura.
- Symmetry for persistent protection.

Prefer brighter, clearer, or more stable compositions than debuffs, while matching the actual mechanic.

## Debuff

Communicate restriction, corruption, vulnerability, decay, suppression, or loss.

Useful motifs:
- Chains or binding hands.
- Cracked armor.
- Broken weapon.
- Withering plant.
- Diseased wound.
- Draining tendrils.
- Downward or inward collapse.
- Obscured eye or silenced mouth.

Do not rely on a skull unless death, mortality, disease, or lethal danger is central to the mechanic.

## Passive

Communicate a persistent relationship between two ideas.

Use a simple compound symbol:
- Weapon plus heart for life steal.
- Shield plus flame for fire resistance.
- Eye plus arrow for accuracy.
- Boot plus wind for movement speed.
- Broken chain plus silhouette for control resistance.

Keep passives simpler and less explosive than active abilities.

## Item

Present the actual object as the hero subject.

Use:
- Three-quarter view.
- Strong silhouette.
- Exaggerated proportions.
- Distinct material lighting.
- Minimal atmospheric background.

Show one item or one clearly unified bundle. Do not add a character unless required to understand scale or use.

## Consumable

Emphasize container, ingredient, and effect.

Examples:
- Potion bottle with a concentrated glow.
- Food with steam or a restorative aura.
- Scroll with a magical seal.

Avoid cluttered still-life arrangements.

## Quest item

Make the object distinctive and narratively important without using text.

Use:
- Unique silhouette.
- Strong material or magical signature.
- Slightly more ceremonial framing or lighting.

## Resource or crafting material

Use one clear stack, chunk, bundle, or container.

Prioritize:
- Material identification.
- Countability.
- Clean silhouette.
- Less dramatic action than abilities.

## Status or crowd-control effect

Show the affected condition rather than a generic magic burst.

Examples:
- Frozen: face or limb encased in angular ice.
- Stunned: impacted helm, stars, or fractured awareness motif.
- Rooted: boot or ankle bound by roots.
- Silenced: mouth sealed by rune, cloth, or shadow.
- Blinded: eye obscured by flash or dark band.

## Class or school family

Use recurring visual grammar rather than identical palettes.

Examples:
- Warrior: steel, leather, crimson, forceful diagonals.
- Shamanic nature: stone, wood, turquoise, amber, elemental rings.
- Arcane: violet, blue-white, geometric runes, concentrated cores.
- Frost: pale blue, white, dark navy, angular crystal forms.
- Fire: orange, red, black, bright yellow-white impact points.
- Holy: ivory, gold, blue, radiant symmetry.
- Shadow: violet-black, desaturated flesh, sharp magenta accents.
- Poison: yellow-green, dark teal, venomous shapes, oily highlights.

Treat these as starting grammars, not rigid mandatory palettes.
